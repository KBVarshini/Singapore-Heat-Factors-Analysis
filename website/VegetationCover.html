Vegetation Cover

Vegetation cover represents the amount and distribution of greenery and vegetation within the country, in this case Singapore. It includes parks, gardens, and also man-made green spaces integrated into the urban landscape.

Singapore's focus on being a "City in a Garden" has led to the incorporation of vegetation to improve air quality, reduce heat, provide habitats, and enhance residents' well-being. Monitoring vegetation cover helps track changes and supports conservation and urban planning efforts.

To calculate the Vegetation Cover for different areas in Singapore, I am using the dataset from Google Earth Engine, using LandSat 8, which provides accurate data.

NDVI Range (approx.) Land cover
Below 0Water, clouds or snow (non-vegetated)
0 to 0.2Bare soil, rock or built-up areas
0.2 to 0.5Thin or moderate vegetation
Above 0.5Dense, healthy vegetation



The limitation of using Landsat 8 satellite is that it takes pictures of large areas and each picture pixel covers a sizable region. When I tried to filter the pictures to show only Singapore, some neighbouring areas of Malaysia are still included in the map, because of the pixel size. Factors like clouds can hide parts of Singapore in some images. Unfortunately, due to the satellite's limitations, we can't completely fix this issue using LandSat 8 and some data from outside Singapore may appear in the images. However, the line chart is accurate as it only calculates the vegetation cover for Singapore and not any neighbouring parts.
